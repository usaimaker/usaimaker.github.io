Three rules for QR codes that scan in the real world

Keep contrast high. Dark modules on a light background. Inverted or pastel-on-pastel codes fail on real phone cameras even when they look fine on a monitor.

Do not oversize the logo. Cover roughly 20% of the area at most, and use error correction level H. A logo that eats the finder patterns makes the code unreadable.

Test on a physical phone before printing. Check the final printed size at the distance people will actually scan from — a 2 cm code on a wall poster will not work.

Static or dynamic?

A static QR encodes the final destination directly, so it never expires and costs nothing to maintain, but you must reprint if the URL changes. A dynamic QR points at a redirect you control, which lets you swap the destination and measure scans. For a printed menu or a business card, link a stable URL you own and you get most of the benefit without a subscription.
