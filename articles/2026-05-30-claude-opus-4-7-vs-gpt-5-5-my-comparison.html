Claude Opus 4.7 vs GPT-5.5: My comparison

I've spent the last month putting Claude Opus 4.7 and GPT-5.5 through their paces, and I'm still trying to figure out which one is the better tool for my writing workflow. My goal is to boost my productivity, and I need a reliable AI assistant to help me get there. I've tried using both tools to generate ideas, outline articles, and even write entire drafts, with mixed results.

My Workflow

I typically start my day by brainstorming ideas for my blog, and that's where Claude Opus 4.7 really shines - it's incredibly good at suggesting topics and angles that I wouldn't have thought of otherwise. For example, last week I was struggling to come up with a new idea for my productivity series, and Claude Opus 4.7 suggested I write about the benefits of using a paper planner, which ended up being a huge hit with my readers. On the other hand, GPT-5.5 is more of a utility player - it's great at helping me with research and fact-checking, but it's not as strong when it comes to generating new ideas.

I've been using GPT-5.5 to help me with my research, and it's been a huge time-saver - I can ask it to find specific information or summarize long articles, and it always delivers. However, I've noticed that it can be a bit too thorough at times, providing me with way more information than I need, which can be overwhelming. I've had to develop a system to filter out the noise and focus on the key points, which has taken some trial and error.

The Not-So-Good Stuff

One of my biggest frustrations with Claude Opus 4.7 is its tendency to produce overly generic responses - sometimes it feels like it's just regurgitating the same old cliches and tropes, without really adding any new insights or perspectives. I've had to learn to prompt it in very specific ways to get the results I need, which can be a bit of a challenge. On the other hand, GPT-5.5 can be a bit too literal at times - I've had instances where I've asked it to summarize a complex topic, and it's given me a response that's way too detailed and technical.

A Honest Moment

I have to admit, I made a mistake last week when I was using GPT-5.5 to help me write a draft - I didn't fact-check its responses carefully enough, and I ended up publishing an article with a few errors. It was a painful lesson to learn, but it's taught me to be more careful and vigilant when working with AI tools. I've since developed a system to double-check the facts and figures provided by GPT-5.5, which has given me more confidence in its abilities.

Head-to-Head Comparison

When it comes to writing actual content, I've found that Claude Opus 4.7 is generally more effective - it's able to capture my voice and tone in a way that GPT-5.5 can't quite match. However, GPT-5.5 is way faster and more efficient when it comes to research and drafting - I can ask it to produce a rough outline or summary in a fraction of the time it would take me to do it myself. For example, I used GPT-5.5 to help me research and outline a series of articles on productivity hacks, and it was able to provide me with a comprehensive outline in under an hour, which saved me a ton of time and effort.

I've been experimenting with using both tools in tandem - I'll use Claude Opus 4.7 to generate ideas and outlines, and then use GPT-5.5 to help me with the actual writing and research. It's been a bit of a learning curve, but I think I'm starting to get the hang of it - my productivity has increased significantly, and I'm able to produce high-quality content more quickly and efficiently. I'm excited to see where this journey takes me, and I'm looking forward to continuing to experiment and refine my workflow.
