My Secret to Consistent AI-Generated Images

I've spent countless hours tinkering with AI image generators, and I've finally cracked the code to getting consistent results. My latest project, a fantasy novel, required a consistent style of illustrations, and I was determined to use AI to produce them - I mean, who needs human artists, right? Well, it turns out, getting consistent AI-generated images is not as easy as I thought, but I've learned a thing or two.

The Early Days

I started by experimenting with different AI models, trying to find the one that worked best for my project. I tried Midjourney, DALL-E, and even some of the lesser-known models, but the results were all over the place - sometimes amazing, sometimes laughable. I was frustrated, to say the least, and I wondered if I was just wasting my time.

My breakthrough came when I stumbled upon a forum post about the importance of prompt engineering. I had been using simple prompts, like "fantasy castle," but the post suggested that more detailed prompts, like "a medieval castle with towers and a moat, in the style of Frank Frazetta," would yield better results. I tried it, and sure enough, the images improved dramatically.

The Power of Prompt Engineering

I spent hours crafting the perfect prompts, and it paid off. My AI-generated images started to look like they were created by a single artist, rather than a committee of robots. I was thrilled, but I knew I couldn't stop there - I needed to refine my process further. I started keeping a notebook of my prompts, along with the results, so I could track what worked and what didn't.

One of my favorite prompts was for a character illustration - I described the character's clothes, hair, and accessories in excruciating detail, and the AI produced an image that was almost perfect. Almost. I had to make some tweaks, like adjusting the color palette and adding some textures, but it was a huge time-saver. My honest moment: I have to admit, I still struggle with getting the AI to produce consistent skin tones - it's like they're either too pale or too orange.

The Importance of Iteration

I've learned that getting consistent AI-generated images is all about iteration. You can't just prompt the AI once and expect perfect results - you need to refine your prompts, try different models, and tweak the output. It's a time-consuming process, but it's worth it. I've developed a workflow that involves generating multiple images, selecting the best ones, and then refining them further.

For example, I was working on an illustration of a dragon, and the AI produced some amazing images, but they were all slightly different. I selected the best one, and then I used it as a reference to generate new images, tweaking the prompt to get the desired result. It took a few iterations, but I finally got an image that I was happy with. My only frustration is that the AI sometimes gets stuck in a loop, producing the same image over and over - I'm like, "Come on, AI, can't you see I'm trying to create something new here?"

My Current Workflow

My current workflow involves a combination of prompt engineering, iteration, and post-processing. I use a mix of AI models, depending on the project, and I've developed a set of favorite prompts that I can rely on. I've also learned to use the AI's limitations to my advantage - for example, I know that Midjourney is great for landscapes, but not so great for portraits.

I've been using my AI-generated images to create a consistent visual style for my novel, and it's been a huge success. My beta readers have commented on the cohesive look and feel of the illustrations, and I've even had a few people ask if I hired a professional artist. My response? I just smile and say it's all about the power of AI - and a lot of hard work.

I'm still experimenting with new AI models and techniques, and I'm excited to see where this technology will take us. For now, I'm just happy to have a workflow that produces consistent results, and I'm enjoying the creative process of working with AI. It's not perfect, but it's getting closer, and that's all that matters.
