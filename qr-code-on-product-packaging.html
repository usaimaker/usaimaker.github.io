QR Codes on Product Packaging: Print Specifications

Packaging is the hardest place to print a QR code. The artwork may be shrunk to fit a label, wrapped around a curved surface, or covered with a gloss varnish that changes the contrast under shop lighting.

What to get right

Agree a minimum module size with the printer (roughly 0.4 mm for hand-held scanning), keep a four-module quiet zone clear of text and borders, and avoid placing the code across a seam or a fold. If a logo sits in the centre, use error correction level H and keep it small.

A checklist before you print

Request a wet proof and scan it under the same lighting customers will use. Matt varnish usually scans better than high gloss, and a dark-on-light code is far more reliable than an inverted one. Export SVG so the modules stay sharp at the final print size.

FAQ

How small can a printed QR code be?

For a phone held about 30 cm away, 2 cm square is a practical minimum for short URLs. Larger codes are needed for long payloads such as vCards; always test the actual printed piece.

Do QR codes expire?

The code itself never expires. What expires is the destination: a link that is deleted, a PDF moved to a new path, or a short-link service that shuts down. Use a URL you control.
